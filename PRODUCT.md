# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mixed, professionals first. The primary reader is a public health surveillance
officer or field epidemiologist scanning for signals in their region: they
tolerate density, they know the vocabulary, and they care most about where a
claim came from and when. A motivated non-specialist, a journalist or an
informed reader, should still be able to work through the same page without a
glossary. Design for the professional's speed, but never at the cost of the
non-specialist's comprehension.

Context of use: a browser tab kept open through a working day, revisited in
short bursts, often alongside other reporting. Rarely a leisurely read.

## Product Purpose

EpiSignal turns public health reporting into traceable events. It watches
official reporting and local news, decides what is worth attention before it is
confirmed, and keeps the distinction between what is interesting and what is
proven.

Every claim keeps the source that made it, the time it was made, and the
observation history that followed. Success is a reader trusting the page enough
to act on it, and being able to check it themselves in one click.

## Brand Personality

Luxury, minimal, editorial. Calm and authoritative rather than urgent. The voice
is a wire service, not an alerting system: it states what was reported, by whom,
and when, then stops. Confidence comes from restraint and from showing the
evidence, never from emphasis or alarm.

Three words: measured, traceable, uncluttered.

Reference feel: Reuters Graphics, Financial Times, Monocle. Premium global
journalism crossed with epidemic intelligence. Never copy these directly.

## Anti-references

- SaaS dashboards. No metric-card grids, no KPI tiles, no chrome around every
  value.
- "Medical blue" health products. The category reflex of white plus teal plus a
  stethoscope icon.
- Glassmorphism, gradients used decoratively, glowing or neon accents.
- Navy as the dark theme background. Dark means true black here.
- Cards around every list item. Separation comes from type and space.
- Heavy border radius. Restraint over rounding.
- Sparkle icons, "AI Summary" labels, or any framing that foregrounds the model
  over the reporting.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence before assertion.** Never show a number without the evidence
   behind it. Every claim carries its source, and the source is one click away.
2. **Preserve uncertainty.** Keep the line between interesting and proven
   visible. Never infer epidemiological milestones or linkage the sources do not
   support; label derived relationships as derived.
3. **Type and space carry hierarchy.** Not boxes, not colour, not chrome. If a
   border can be removed without losing meaning, remove it.
4. **Restraint is the credibility signal.** This product earns trust by refusing
   to look like the dashboards it competes with. Quiet is the position.
5. **Colour is semantic or absent.** Colour encodes disease group, recency or
   status, and always has a text equivalent beside it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at 4.5:1 or better in both themes, full keyboard
operation, visible focus on every interactive element, semantic headings, and
`prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Icon-only controls carry accessible names.
No information is conveyed by colour alone: disease group, recency and status
each pair their colour with a text label.
